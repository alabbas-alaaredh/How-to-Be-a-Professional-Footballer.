03 / Build the basics

Small touches.

Better decisions.

Ball mastery means keeping the ball under control with different parts of your feet. Combine it with looking up and choosing when to pass.

A

B

PRACTICE IDEA / Pass to a teammate, then move into a new space.

01 · Dribbling & ball control

Use gentle inside and outside touches to keep the ball close. Move through a line of cones with both feet. Look up between touches, then change direction and accelerate into space.

Try it: Walk through the course first. Add speed only when you can keep control.

02 · Passing & first touch

Pass to a wall with the inside of your foot. Receive across your body and return the ball with your other foot.

03 · Shooting & finishing

Aim for a corner of the goal from close range. Practise with both feet and add distance once you can hit your target consistently.

04 · Scanning & decision-making

Glance over your shoulder before receiving. Pick your next pass or direction before the ball reaches you.

05 / The player behind the performance

Build the habits behind your game.

Progress also means learning how to respond, how to work with others, and how to begin again.

01 / DISCIPLINE

Keep small promises.

Be on time, prepare your kit and listen to feedback. A realistic routine should leave room for school and rest.

Example to try: Pack your bag the night before. Choose one practice goal before you step onto the pitch.

02 / MOTIVATION

Focus on the next step.

Some sessions will feel difficult. Set a small goal you can control, and compare your progress with your own earlier efforts.

Example to try: After losing possession, identify what happened. Practise that situation instead of deciding you are “bad at football.”

03 / ATTITUDE & CONFIDENCE

Be a teammate first.

Confidence grows through preparation and learning. Encourage others, accept useful criticism, and keep asking for the ball after a mistake.

Example to try: If a teammate misses a chance, say “next one.” If your coach corrects you, ask what to change on the next attempt.

06 / Know the game

Laws & rules, made simple.

A beginner’s guide to association football, based on IFAB’s 2026/27 Laws. Youth competitions can use modified formats.

01

Match basics

A match is played by two teams, each with up to eleven players, including a goalkeeper.

02

Offside

Offside position only becomes an offence when a player becomes involved in active play.

03

Fouls & handball

The referee awards free kicks for offences, including certain handball offences.

04

Yellow & red cards

A yellow card is a caution. A red card sends the player off.

05

Free kicks & penalties

A penalty is awarded for a direct-free-kick offence by a defender inside their own penalty area.

06

Throw-ins

A throw-in restarts play when the whole ball crosses the touchline.

07

Goal kicks & corners

A goal kick or corner is awarded according to who last touched the ball before it crossed the goal line without a goal.

08

Goalkeeper rules

Goalkeepers may handle the ball inside their own penalty area, subject to the Laws of the Game.

Learn the rules. Play with respect.

These are short explanations. Your competition’s rules and the full Laws provide the detail.

07 / Take your next step

From practice to playing opportunities.

Join a local team, play regularly, and ask a qualified coach for honest feedback. Work on one weakness at a time. As you develop, explore suitable club or academy trials with a parent or guardian and check opportunities directly with the club. Professional football is highly competitive; consistent learning helps you develop, but no routine guarantees a contract.
